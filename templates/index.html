Econometric Methodology & Analytical Limitations

1. Constant vs. Variable Elasticity & Interior Revenue Optimum

In basic microeconomics, constant-elasticity demand Q(P) = A · Pb produces revenue R(P) = A · P1+b. Because this function is strictly monotonic for any zero-cost framework, an unbounded interior revenue maximum does not exist mathematically.

Our Solution: We deploy a Log-Quadratic Demand Model:
ln(Q) = β₀ + β₁·ln(P) + β₂·(ln(P))²
When β₂ < 0 (increasing price sensitivity at higher price levels), revenue exhibits a true interior global maximum at:
P* = exp( -(1 + β₁) / (2·β₂) )

2. Microeconomic Profit Maximization (Lerner Markup Rule)

To model realistic commercial scenarios where unit marginal cost c is non-zero, the platform evaluates the profit function:
Π(P) = (P - c) · Q(P)
Under constant elasticity |b| > 1, the optimal price is given analytically by the classical Lerner index markup equation:
P* = c · ( |b| / (|b| - 1) )

3. Identification & Confounding Sins in Observational Retail Data

  • Simultaneity & Endogeneity: Observed prices reflect both demand willingness-to-pay and unobserved cost shocks. Without exogenous instrumental variables (IV), OLS slopes measure associative price sensitivity rather than pure causal elasticity.
  • Wholesale Tiering: Bulk order discounts can artificially generate positive elasticity slopes if large business buyers purchase high volumes at preferential price tiers. Such items are flagged as Anomalous / Confounded.
  • Supply Curve Transparency: Point-of-sale datasets omit supplier marginal cost schedules. The market clearing module uses an explicit simulation benchmark (assumed εs = 0.8) and is clearly labeled as assumption-based.
